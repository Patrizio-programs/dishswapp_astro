01  ·  Welcome

About this book

Edition One · Nov 2026

DishSwapp is an open-source collaborative recipe site — a place where home cooks collect, swap and improve recipes together. The current site is a React + Bootstrap 5 single-page app backed by Sanity CMS and Clerk auth. It works, but the visual layer has accreted one component at a time: the same two custom properties (--pink --blue) repeated across eleven CSS files, a light/dark toggle bolted onto a Bootstrap jumbotron, and a single tagline doing all the heavy lifting.

This book is the design source-of-truth for the makeover. It keeps what already works — the peachy-pink #F9B7A4 and deep teal #3D717C, the Playfair headlines, the warm, kitchen-table voice — and extends them into a full, token-driven system that survives a real framework migration to Astro + Bootstrap 5.3.

How to use this book
  • Sections 02–04 — read once, share with new contributors. Sets mission, voice, personality.
  • Sections 05–09 — the visual identity. Reference when designing any new page or component.
  • Sections 10–12 — component specs. Implement verbatim in Astro components.
  • Sections 13–14 — iconography and photography. Hand to icon designers and photo editors.
  • Sections 15–16 — voice and messaging. Reference when writing UI copy.
  • Sections 17–19 — mockups. Use as visual contract for the implementation.
  • Section 20 — ready-to-paste tokens. Drop straight into src/styles/tokens.scss.
A note on scope
This document does not specify the Sanity content model, Clerk auth flows, or deployment. It is a brand & front-end design system only.
02  ·  Brand foundation

Mission, vision, values

Why DishSwapp exists, and what it stands for.
Mission
A shared kitchen, not a walled garden.

DishSwapp helps home cooks discover, save and improve recipes together — out in the open, for free, forever. Every recipe is a starting point; every cook is a potential contributor.

Vision
The cookbook the internet writes together.

A world where the best version of any recipe is the one a hundred cooks have already improved on — with notes, swaps and step-by-step photos that an algorithm could never have written.

Four values
Open

Source, content and roadmap are public. We default to sharing. We never paywall what the community built.

Behaves like: a public roadmap, RFCs in the repo, MIT-licensed code.
Collaborative

Recipes are improved in public. Forks, comments and version history are first-class, not afterthoughts.

Behaves like: inline comments on steps, “I tried this” threads.
Curious

We treat every cuisine, diet and skill level as equally worth exploring. No snobbery, no gatekeeping.

Behaves like: cuisines side-by-side, not ranked; beginner-friendly language.
Generous

We credit, we link back, we assume good faith. The product is a gift, not a funnel.

Behaves like: author bylines, source links on adapted recipes, no dark patterns.

Clear-space

Maintain at least 1× cap-height of clear space around the wordmark on every side. Nothing — not photography, not other UI, not the page edge — may enter this zone.

Minimum size

24 px digital for the wordmark. The symbol alone can go down to 16 px (favicon). Print: 20 mm wide. Below these, the swap-loop detail collapses.

Don’t

Don’t recolor the wordmark outside the documented palette. Don’t stretch, rotate, or add drop-shadows. Don’t place the ink wordmark on a photo without the cream/teal plate behind it.

05  ·  Color — primary palette

A refined, extended family

The two colors you already had, plus the supporting cast they always needed.

08  ·  Typography — Bootstrap 5 mapping

Drop-in SCSS overrides

Paste this before importing Bootstrap to wire the type system in.
// src/styles/_type.scss — loaded BEFORE Bootstrap’s _variables.scss $font-family-sans-serif: 'Inter', system-ui, sans-serif; $font-family-serif: 'Playfair Display', Georgia, serif; $font-family-monospace: 'JetBrains Mono', ui-monospace, monospace; $font-size-base: 1rem; // 16px root $font-size-lead: 1.0625rem; // 17px $headings-font-family: $font-family-serif; $headings-font-weight: 700; $headings-color: $ds-ink; $h1-font-size: 3rem; // 48px $h2-font-size: 2rem; // 32px $h3-font-size: 1.375rem; // 22px $h4-font-size: 1rem; // 16px — Inter bold $lead-font-size: 1.0625rem; $lead-font-weight: 400; $link-color: $ds-teal; $link-hover-color: $ds-teal-dark; $link-decoration: underline; $link-hover-decoration: underline; // Tabular figures for every numeric — recipe-critical @mixin tnum { font-feature-settings: 'tnum' 1; } .recipe-quantity, .timer, .rating { @include tnum; }
When to use serif vs sans
  • Serif (Playfair) — recipe titles, page H1s, pull-quotes, section titles, the wordmark. Italic for emphasis or pull-quotes only.
  • Sans (Inter) — everything else. Body copy, UI, buttons, captions, meta. Never use serif inside a button or a nav item.
  • Mono (JetBrains) — ingredient quantities, timers, ratings, code, the eyebrow labels in this book.
Pairing rules
  • One serif headline per page — never two competing H1s.
  • Serif size must always be ≥1.4× the next-largest sans on the page. Don’t let a 22px Playfair sit next to a 16px Inter.
  • Never set body copy in Playfair — it’s a display face. Body in Playfair is the #1 amateur typography mistake.
  • Line-height for Playfair is 1.05–1.20. For Inter body, 1.55–1.65.

13  ·  Imagery & art direction

Photography that looks like a real kitchen

Warm light, real plates, hands in frame. No stock-photo plastic.

All photography is treated with the same warm grade — lifted shadows, warm whites, soft contrast — so that contributed photos from many cameras sit together on the page without looking like a collage. The color treatment does the heavy lifting; the photographer only needs to capture real food on real plates.

Hero · 16:9
Top-down or 45°, full-bleed. One dish, no props except the plate.
Cuisine card · 4:3
A signature dish for the cuisine, single subject, warm tone.
Step · 1:1
Hands and pan in frame. Shows the action, not the result.
Author · 1:1
In their kitchen, natural light, no logo overlay. Real person.
Do
  • Natural light, near a window. No flash, no ring-light.
  • Real plates, real cutlery, real surfaces. The point is authenticity.
  • Hands in frame where it makes sense — stirring, plating, tearing bread.
  • Crop tight on the food. The dish is the subject, not the kitchen.
  • Warm white balance. Cream should always look cream, not blue.
Don’t
  • Stock photo plastic food — glossy, perfect, unattainable.
  • Harsh flash or any blue-white color temperature.
  • Cluttered backgrounds, dirty dishes, brand-name packaging.
  • Filters that crush blacks or push saturation past the warm grade.
  • Watermarks from recipe blogs. Credit the author in the byline instead.
Crop ratios
Hero 16:9 · Cuisine card 4:3 · Recipe card 16:10 · Step 1:1 · Author 1:1 · Social share 4:5 (Instagram portrait). Never crop a 16:9 hero to a 1:1 step image — reshoot at the right ratio.

15  ·  Tagline & messaging

Keep the original, with three alternatives on the bench

The three-word tagline stays primary. The others cover different contexts.
Current · keep
Collect, Collaborate, Cook.
The original three-word tagline. It already names the three core actions on the site, mirrors the alliteration that makes it stick, and reads naturally as a verb sequence. Don’t replace it without a stronger reason than “it feels old”.
Use: homepage hero, footer, app store description
Alt A
Cook together, one swap at a time.
Leads with the verb, then explains the mechanic. Better for new visitors who don’t yet know what “swapping” means in this context.
Use: onboarding screen 1, pitch deck
Alt B
Recipes that get passed around.
Wry, warm, community-flavored. The double meaning (passed around a table / passed around online) is the joke.
Use: social bios, contributor recruitment page
Alt C
Your kitchen, our cookbook.
Foregrounds the user’s ownership of the cooking while naming the shared artifact. Best on the cookbook page itself.
Use: cookbook empty state, “Sign in to your cookbook” screen

Italian · 45 min · Serves 6
Grandma’s Sunday ragù
by Patrizio · updated 3 days ago · 142 cooks tried this
Ingredients
400 g   beef chuck
200 g   pork shoulder
1   onion, finely diced
2   carrots, finely diced
120 ml   whole milk
200 ml   dry white wine
400 g   canned tomatoes
Tip
The milk softens the meat. Don’t skip it — it’s the trick everyone asks about.
Method
1
Sweat the soffritto in olive oil until translucent, about 7 minutes. Don’t brown.
2
Add the diced meat and brown on all sides. Season with salt and a fine grate of nutmeg.
3
Pour in the milk and let it reduce until almost gone. This takes patience — 15 min.
4
Add the wine and reduce by half. Then the tomatoes, a pinch of sugar, and the lowest possible heat.
5
Cook at a bare simmer for 3–4 hours. Stir occasionally. Toss with fresh tagliatelle.
